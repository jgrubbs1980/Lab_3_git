The leash clinked against Barnaby’s collar, a tiny metallic chime that instantly set off a cyclone of thumping tail and skittering paws. Barnaby, a golden retriever whose enthusiasm vastly outweighed his discipline, pressed his nose against the front door, snorting impatient little huffs of condensation onto the glass.

The autumn air hit sharp and cool the moment the door swung open. Barnaby took the porch steps in a single, ungraceful leap, dragging the leash taut. Lawn-mowing season was over, but the neighborhood smelled alive—wet pavement, rotting maple leaves, and the distant, agonizing scent of someone else’s evening bacon.

Barnaby had a route, though "route" implied a plan. Really, it was a chaotic zig-zag driven entirely by olfactory whimsy. First came the fire hydrant at the corner, which required four distinct, meticulous sniffs before earning a brief, casual leg-lift. Next was the oak tree where a squirrel lived, a tree Barnaby approached with the dramatic, slow-motion stealth of a lion stalking a gazelle on the Serengeti. The squirrel chattered a bitter insult from ten feet up, and Barnaby barked once—a booming, chesty sound that startled a flock of sparrows into the dusk sky.

Halfway down the block, the streetlight hummed to life, casting a warm yellow circle on the asphalt. Barnaby stopped dead in his tracks, his ears perking up as a neighbor jogged past with a quick wave. He leaned into the leash, panting happily, fully convinced the runner was executing a drill solely for his entertainment.

On the loop back, the pace slowed. Barnaby trotted at a gentle heel, his coat catching the last crimson light fading behind the rooftops. As the front porch light came back into view, he turned around, sat heavy on his haunches, and offered one soft, expectant whine—just checking to see if a second lap was on the table.
