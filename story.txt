The leash clinked against Barnaby’s collar, a tiny metallic chime that instantly set off a cyclone of thumping tail and skittering paws. Barnaby, a golden retriever whose enthusiasm vastly outweighed his discipline, pressed his nose against the front door, snorting impatient little huffs of condensation onto the glass.

The autumn air hit sharp and cool the moment the door swung open. Barnaby took the porch steps in a single, ungraceful leap, dragging the leash taut. Lawn-mowing season was over, but the neighborhood smelled alive—wet pavement, rotting maple leaves, and the distant, agonizing scent of someone else’s evening bacon.

Barnaby had a route, though "route" implied a plan. Really, it was a chaotic zig-zag driven entirely by olfactory whimsy. First came the fire hydrant at the corner, which required four distinct, meticulous sniffs before earning a brief, casual leg-lift. Next was the oak tree where a squirrel lived, a tree Barnaby approached with the dramatic, slow-motion stealth of a lion stalking a gazelle on the Serengeti.

The squirrel chattered a bitter insult from ten feet up, but before Barnaby could even bark, the wind shifted. A low, ominous honking echoed from the fog rolling off the local pond.

Out of the dusk, an airborne vanguard of six massive Canada geese descended like a tactical strike force. They didn’t just fly by; they targeted the sidewalk with terrifying precision. Hissing like leaky steam pipes, wings flared to a six-foot span, the feathered menace hit the asphalt in full battle formation.

The lion of the Serengeti instantly downgraded to a terrified puddle of golden fur. Barnaby let out a shrill, unmanly squeak and bolted backward, wrapping the leash three times around my legs and taking us both down in a pile of wet leaves. The alpha goose hissed louder, snaking its neck forward in an unmistakable threat display. Abandoning all dignity, I scrambled backward on my hands and knees, dragging a scrambling, 80-pound dog behind me like a giant, panic-stricken sack of potatoes until we managed to retreat into the safety of the porch.

EVERYONE DIED.
